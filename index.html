Ask the daisy when

The old counting rhyme goes this year, next year, sometime, never. Ask it a question, then take the rays off one at a time. Whatever the last ray lands on is your answer.

press a ray to startthirteen on it, four words in the rhyme

People were settling questions with this flower long before it had a settled English name. You take the rays off one at a time — this year, next year, sometime, never — and whatever you land on at the end is allowed to count as an answer.

You could count the rays first and do the arithmetic and know before you started. Nobody counts them first. That is most of the reason people keep playing it.

“Daisy” is a worn-down dæges ēage — day's eye — for the way the whole head shuts at night and opens in the morning. Chaucer wrote it out in full: the eye of the day.
